# LF5 Scenarios

Choose **one** of these scenarios for your team. Each Epic (Holistic Task) will be processed in the context of your chosen scenario ("Zoom-In").

## Scenario A: Neustadt Museum (The Heritage Project)

- **Industry:** Public Sector / Cultural Heritage
- **Initial Situation:** The "Neustadt Museum" holds over 50,000 artifacts ranging from Roman coins to medieval armor. The documentation is an absolute nightmare. Past curators used different categorization systems, languages, and analog formats (books, loose paper, index cards). Some items are missing critical data like "Year of Origin" or "Current Location".
- **The Mission:** The museum received a grant to digitize its inventory. You must analyze their chaotic paper trails, design a logical database structure, and build a prototype application that allows staff to securely input and search for artifacts.
- **Your Main Stakeholder:** **Dr. Elara Vance (Head Curator)**. She is a brilliant historian but has zero technical knowledge. She is terrified that a "computer crash" will delete history. She needs simple interfaces, extreme data safety, and clear communication without IT jargon.
- **Special Challenge:** Handling incomplete data records and mapping physical storage locations (Room -> Shelf -> Display Case) digitally.

## Scenario B: Mutti's Recipe Collection (The Bakery Scale-Up)

- **Industry:** Food & Beverage / Retail
- **Initial Situation:** "Mutti's Backstube" is a local legend. They are expanding to three new locations. However, the business relies on founder Martha's secret recipes, which are stored in a physical, flour-dusted binder. The recipes use inconsistent units ("2 cups of flour", "500g sugar", "a large pinch of salt") and lack precise scaling factors for industrial production.
- **The Mission:** You must translate this unstructured, analog knowledge into a strict digital format. You will design a system that normalizes ingredients, calculates nutritional values, and allows bakers to scale recipes from 10 to 1,000 portions automatically.
- **Your Main Stakeholder:** **Martha "Mutti" Klein (Founder & Master Baker)**. She is skeptical of "the cloud" and fears her recipes will be stolen. She values tradition over efficiency but understands the business need. The solution must be foolproof for tired kitchen staff working at 3 AM.
- **Special Challenge:** Standardizing highly variable analog data (measurements) and ensuring the digital prototype is usable in a messy kitchen environment (e.g., via a tablet interface).

## Scenario C: Metropolis Library (The Library Rescue)

- **Industry:** Public Service / Education
- **Initial Situation:** Disaster struck the "Metropolis Library". Their 15-year-old proprietary management software suffered a catastrophic failure. While the raw database backups were mostly saved, the software itself is dead. The staff is currently tracking book loans, returns, and late fees using huge, chaotic Excel spreadsheets that are constantly out of sync.
- **The Mission:** You need to design a modern, relational system from scratch. You must map out the complex relationships between Books, Authors, Categories, Members, and Loans. Afterwards, you will set up a development environment to start building a robust, modern replacement.
- **Your Main Stakeholder:** **Mr. Arthur Pendelton (Head Librarian)**. He is a strict, highly organized man who loves rules, constraints, and perfect order. He expects the new system to prevent any logical errors (e.g., a member borrowing a book that is already marked as "lost").
- **Special Challenge:** Designing a complex relational data model (ERM) that handles edge cases like overdue fees, multiple copies of the same book (ISBN tracking), and member privilege levels.
