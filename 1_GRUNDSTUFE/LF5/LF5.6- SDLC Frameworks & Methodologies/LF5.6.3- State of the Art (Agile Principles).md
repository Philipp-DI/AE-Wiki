# LF5.6.3: State of the Art (Agile Principles)

## User Story

As a Scrum Master, **I** want to master the Agile Manifesto and the Scrum framework, so that **I** can guide my team through iterative Sprints, daily stand-ups, and continuous improvement.

## Celebration Criteria

- **I can define** the 4 core values of the Agile Manifesto. (K1)
- **I know how to identify** the 3 roles, 3 artifacts, and 5 events in Scrum. (K2)
- **I can outline** the chronological flow of a Scrum Sprint. (K3)

## Knowledge Briefing

Agile is a mindset. **Scrum** is a specific framework that applies that mindset.

**1\. The Agile Manifesto (2001) - 4 Core Values:**

- Individuals and interactions _over_ processes and tools.
- Working software _over_ comprehensive documentation.
- Customer collaboration _over_ contract negotiation.
- Responding to change _over_ following a plan.

**2\. The Scrum Framework:**

- **3 Roles:** Product Owner (focuses on WHAT), Scrum Master (focuses on HOW the team works), Developers (build the software). _There are no "Project Managers" in Scrum!_
- **3 Artifacts:** Product Backlog (the master wish list), Sprint Backlog (what we do _this_ week), Increment (the usable piece of software).
- **5 Events:** The Sprint (the container, usually 2 weeks), Sprint Planning, Daily Scrum (15 min stand-up), Sprint Review (demo to customer), Sprint Retrospective (team reflects on how to improve).

## Common Pitfalls

- Treating the Daily Scrum as a "status report to the boss". It is a synchronization meeting _for the developers_, not for management.

## Mandatory Tasks

1. List the 4 core values of the Agile Manifesto. (K1)
2. Name the 3 official roles within a Scrum Team. (K1)
3. Explain the difference between the "Product Backlog" and the "Sprint Backlog". (K2)
4. Describe the purpose of the "Sprint Retrospective" meeting. (K2)
5. Outline a basic timeline (e.g., using a flowchart) showing the order of the 5 Scrum Events during a typical 2-week Sprint. (K3)

## Optional Tasks

1. Analyze a conflict where a CEO demands an urgent new feature be added to the middle of an active, running Sprint. How should the Scrum Master react? (K4)
2. Evaluate the risk of "Scrum-but" (e.g., "We do Scrum, _but_ we don't do Retrospectives because they take too long"). (K5)
3. Design an agenda for an effective Sprint Planning meeting for a completely remote development team. (K6)
